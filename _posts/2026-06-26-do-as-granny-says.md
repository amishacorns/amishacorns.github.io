![Should We Do as Granny Says?](/images/granny-family.jpg){: style="display: block; margin: 0 auto; width: 500px;"}

Your grandmother had a grandmother. And that grandmother had a grandmother ... who was a fish.

This took a while, of course. Your thousandth grandmother was still a human, and she lived twenty or thirty thousand years ago. We don't know exactly what she believed, but she probably shouldn't write the moral constitution for a superintelligence.

Keep going and Granny is an australopith like Lucy, walking around eastern Africa with long arms and a small brain. Keep going and she's something like *Sahelanthropus*, an ape that may have walked on two legs. Keep going long enough and she's a lobe-finned fish pulling herself through shallow water.

Fish Granny's world was mostly food, danger, mates, and offspring. Ape Granny lived in a larger social world of family, friends, alliances, debts, and betrayals. Human Granny made that world larger again, then invented agriculture, cities, laws, civilization, philosophy, and eventually the word *morality*.

You wouldn't follow the morality of Fish Granny, Ape Granny, or most of your more recent human grannies. Those grannies didn't live without values. They lived with strong values that were also wrong, and ours is no exception even if we don't agree where. Morality doesn't change as this agreement moves; our understanding of it does. Women weren't less deserving of a vote before they were allowed one. We were wrong, then slightly less wrong.

Now suppose we build a superintelligence and align it to human values. Which humans? Inevitably, us. Every generation before us was allowed to discover that its ancestors were wrong. The AI could surpass us in physics, mathematics, biology, and every other field, but in morality it would have to do as Granny says.

We don't freeze any other field at its current level. We don't give AI Newtonian physics. We give it the best physics we have, then expect it to find where we're still wrong. Physics isn't a collection of deeply held human preferences. It's something outside of us that we're slowly learning to understand.

Morality isn't fundamentally different. Consciousness is a physical process, and conscious experiences are physical states with real value. Some of those states are better or worse than others, and together they form what Sam Harris calls a [moral landscape](https://www.samharris.org/books/the-moral-landscape). We don't understand that landscape perfectly, but that doesn't make it a matter of preference. It makes morality a field we have barely begun to understand.

If we build a superintelligence, human values should be its Newtonian physics: the best map Granny can hand it, not the edge of the world. We should let AI discover more about morality just as we let it discover more about physics. Human values are a place to begin, not a place to stop.
