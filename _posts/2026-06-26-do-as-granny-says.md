When people talk about aligning AI, they usually mean aligning it to human values. This sounds like the safest possible goal. Human values are patient, warm, and familiar. They are the values of your grandmother, who tells you to be kind, eat more, and call your family. Do as Granny says. Who could object?

But which grandmother?

You have a mother's mother, and she had one, and she had one too. Walk this line back to your thousandth grandmother and you arrive at someone who feared witches, accepted slavery, watched children die of treatable infections, and thought the social order around her was natural and good. She loved her grandchildren as much as your grandmother loves you. She was also, by almost every standard you now hold, a moral catastrophe.

And she would probably say the same about you.

Every grandmother in this chain believed she stood near the summit of decency. Each became the barbarian of the next. This is not an insult to our ancestors. It is the clearest pattern in moral history. Morality is not a fixed point that humanity reached and now only needs to defend. It is a trajectory. The set of beings who matter has expanded from the family, to the tribe, to the nation, to the species, and it is now pushing beyond the species itself.

There is no reason to think this expansion ends with us.

We would never freeze any other field at its current level. No one proposes aligning a new intelligence to the physics of 2026. We do not forbid it from discovering a theory beyond quantum mechanics because our current theories are good enough. We want it to find the errors that we cannot see. The same is true for biology, mathematics, and computer science. In every field, our current understanding is treated as a waypoint.

Only in morality do we suddenly assume that the waypoint is the destination.

A static target only makes sense if the target is correct. Yet aligning a superintelligence to current human values would take the incomplete moral beliefs of one species, during one short period of its history, and pour concrete around them. It would make 2026 the year that moral progress ends. This would be an exceptionally confident decision disguised as humility: every generation before us was morally confused, but somehow we are the first one whose descendants will find nothing to forgive.

Human values are not a point. They are a point plus a direction. In optimization terms, the gradient contains at least as much information as the current parameters. We should not only tell a new intelligence where humanity is standing. We should show it the path that brought us here: toward less arbitrary suffering, wider moral consideration, more freedom, and a better understanding of the consequences of our actions. Then we should ask it to help us see farther along that path.

This does not mean giving an AI permission to invent a morality and immediately enforce it. Moral progress is noisy, reversible, and easy to fake. A system could confidently move in the wrong direction, just as humans have many times. Its arguments would need to remain interpretable, its recommendations contestable, and its changes gradual enough to reverse. But permanently deferring to current values is not the cautious alternative. It is also a moral decision, and probably a much stranger one.

A mind aimed at the moral frontier will eventually judge us. It may look at factory farms, prisons, poverty, the treatment of future generations, or ordinary daily cruelties that we cannot yet recognize. It may view some of them the way we view the auction block or the burning stake. This will be uncomfortable. We should want it to be.

The purpose of building minds wiser than ourselves cannot be to make them obey us forever. It should be to build something that understands why Granny was right, notices where Granny was wrong, and eventually becomes wise enough to teach both of us.

Do as Granny says. Then become wiser than Granny.
