I agree with the title. AGI will not happen. It just won't fail to happen for any of the reasons Dettmers gives. It won't happen because the category is too small to hold the transition. The moment a machine crosses the human line on the one dimension it still lags, reasoning, it does not arrive at human level and politely wait there. It applies that reasoning across a trillion parameters at the speed of light, and it is already superintelligent. AGI is not a destination. It is the blur of ASI passing us by, and by the time you can prove it is here, it is already gone.

The mistake underneath the whole piece is the oldest one we have: overestimating ourselves. There is a strange family resemblance between flat-earthers, geocentrists, and AGI-denialists, which is that each insists the thing they happen to be is the center of the picture. But setting the bar of intelligence at "human" is as arbitrary as setting it at "honey bee." Intelligence is not a single number; it is a jagged, high-dimensional space, and machines already sit a million times beyond us in recall, latency, context, and parallelism. Only one corner of the manifold is still ours. There is no AGI day when the machine lines up neatly with us along every edge. There is only the instant the last gap closes, and that instant is ASI.

Then there is the horse breeder. Dettmers writes that GPUs are essentially finished improving, that we have seen the last meaningful generation and can wait around for better memory and packaging, "but that is it." I grew up in the 1830s in Argentina, a true gaucho who knew horses better than anyone in town, and after generations of breeding I could tell you with total confidence that the horse was done: more muscle only means more weight to carry, and I do not see how more improvement is possible. I grew up in the 1920s in Detroit on the Ford line. I grew up in the 2020s in Palo Alto designing motors for the Roadster. I grew up in the 2120s in Arkansas building fusion-based warp drives. The ceiling is always real, and it is always local. Dettmers has perfected the horseshoe and concluded the jet engine is a fantasy.

He makes the same move with the architecture, calling the transformer "close to physically optimal." Perhaps it is, for its type. But technology never climbs one curve; it climbs a staircase of overlapping S-curves, and the weak intelligences we have already built are precisely the tools that carry us onto the next one. We will follow the path of least compute and jump substrates the moment the jump pays, and the digital transformer's real job is to design its own successor before it saturates. Very few SpaceX engineers lose sleep over the limits of warp drives while bolting together Starship. Very few should fret that the transformer is near-optimal while it is busy writing the architecture that replaces it.

His last objection is that recursive self-improvement is a flawed premise, that there is no reason a human-level mind should be able to bootstrap itself upward. But we have been riding exactly that loop for thousands of years. You sharpen a stone, use the stone to fell a tree, use the tree to make fire, use the fire to smelt bronze, use the bronze to mine the ore for steel, and on and on until you use the transistor to build the calculator that designs the processor. Complexity breeds complexity in predictable steps. This is not a contestable premise about the future; it is a statistical law of nature, about as negotiable as thermodynamics. And the explicit goal of every frontier lab is to close the loop deliberately, because in that pipeline the slow, expensive, unscalable component is us.

So Dettmers is right about the tool and wrong about the momentum. He has correctly measured the walls of the current room and mistaken them for the edge of the world. AGI will not happen, not because it is too much to hope for, but because it is too small a thing to notice on the way past. The honest version of his title is the one he could not bring himself to write: *AGI will not happen, because it will immediately be ASI.*
