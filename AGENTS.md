# Site-Wide Editorial Rules

- Inside figures, use Title Case for concise titles, headings, labels, diagram nodes, and legend items.
- Use sentence case for complete sentences and explanatory prose everywhere, including figure subtitles, annotations, and callouts. Do not mechanically capitalize every word merely because text appears inside a figure.
- Outside figures, use normal capitalization appropriate to the format. Article descriptions, subtitles, captions, and body paragraphs should remain in sentence case unless specifically requested otherwise.
- Never use a wh-clause or interrogative-style headline such as "What X Can Do," "Why X Matters," or "How X Changes Y." This includes titles, headings, figure titles, and callouts. State the subject directly with a declarative phrase or noun phrase.
- For figures, use the site's black, white, gray, and violet palette. Violet is the only default accent; add another color only when it encodes essential meaning that cannot be conveyed by position, labels, shape, fill, or line treatment.
- Never use em dashes. Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses instead.
- Never use corrective binary contrast constructions such as "not X, but Y," "X does not do Y; it does Z," or "this is not X; it is Y." State the positive claim directly.
- Avoid stock rebuttal transitions such as "the obvious objection." Connect paragraphs through the subject and argument already in motion.
- Keep article prose casual and direct. State the supported conclusion in the body, attach a quiet numbered citation, and leave source names and methodological detail in the references section unless the identity of the source is itself important.
